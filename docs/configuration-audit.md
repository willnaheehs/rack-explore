Scope: every one of the 17 loadable catalog platforms, the original four-rack H100 cluster, and the custom builder. This is an audit of the documented models. No production data center has supplied an as-built bill of materials, cable schedule or telemetry.

## What passes and what remains open

Automated checks cover internal inspection, unique identifiers and part keys, nonempty specifications, GPU counts, source metadata, U placement, NVL72 template integrity, reference graph endpoints, and network capacity bounds. Explicit or inferred protocol support and nominal rate limits are enforced in the builder and JSON imports. Slower front-end interfaces cannot satisfy excess 800G compute links. Lenovo's two Ethernet planes share each adapter's aggregate capacity.

Remaining review items are shown in the app: exact CPU/DIMM/SSD/NIC SKUs and populations, numbered port assignments, breakout modes, optics, cable part numbers, switch firmware, rack rails, service clearances, weights, site electrical capacity, A/B redundancy and cooling design. The capacity checker is a necessary-bounds check, not a physical port allocator or vendor configurator. HPE's unselected slots never become verified 800G adapters just because provisional links are drawn. Vendor fabric plans remain distinct from the rack cable schedule.

Vera Rubin NVL72 and AMD Helios remain preliminary catalog entries and cannot load a fabricated rack configuration. The original H100 cluster retains its four racks, 28 devices, 64 GPUs, 137 link groups and all three fabric layers.

## Regression coverage

`tests/config-validation.test.mjs` checks all loadable presets plus the original H100 cluster, independent manufacturer population checkpoints, source conflicts and unknown component counts, invalid and mixed NVL72 templates, protocol/rate incompatibility, overbooked 800G capacity, provisional adapter reviews, import atomicity and structured validation readback. Existing hardware, fabric and power tests remain in place.
